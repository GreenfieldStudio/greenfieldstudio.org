# How Rain on the Cabin Window is drawn

Each frame is drawn in three passes. First, the view outside is painted at a third of the canvas resolution: an overcast night sky with noise clouds, three rows of conifers, ground mist and faint streaks of distant rain. Second, a full-resolution height map of the water on the glass is built from a drop simulation: small beads, larger sliding drops and their thin trails. Third, a composite pass shows the blurred outside through clean glass and a brighter, inverted view inside each drop, with a dark edge, highlights and a faint warm room reflection. Drops land at random from a seeded xoshiro128** generator with 128 bits of state, then merge, slide in stop-and-go runs and evaporate. The backdrop does cycle: the clouds drift round a loop every 40 minutes, the trees sway every 47 seconds and the distant streaks repeat every 60 seconds, so the backdrop alone returns to the same state after about 31 hours. The sound is synthesised live in an AudioWorklet from filtered noise, with no recordings: rain hiss, ticks on the glass, heavier drops and soft wind gusts, following slow seeded random drifts.
