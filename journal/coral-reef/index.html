Making a reef that never loops

The first Greenfield Ambience film is an hour in a coral reef. It is a recording of a world running in real time, not a short clip stretched to fill an hour.

Watch Coral Reef Aquarium Explore Ambience
1:00:00 one hour · 4K 60 fps · no music

Life in the background

The reef is more than a still scene with a camera in front of it. Clownfish settle into anemones, blue tangs and moorish idols pass through the kelp, a lionfish patrols the rocks, and a manta ray glides above. The movement of the water and the changing sunlight hold the scene together while the smaller encounters come and go.

The sound belongs to the same place: deep swell, moving water, bubbles and the crackle of snapping shrimp. There is no music to set the pace for you. The idea is to make a place you can leave on in the background and return to when you want a quieter room.

An hour, not a repeating minute

The scene and soundscape run continuously while the film is captured. Nothing inside that hour is a short video or audio loop repeated to make it longer. The finished film is, of course, a recording: if you replay it, you will see the same hour again.

Capture happens one frame at a time. The world advances, a frame is rendered and encoded, and then the world advances again. That separates the smoothness of the finished 4K, 60 fps film from how long any individual frame took to render.
